# Documentation and writing

Read this playbook if your department is documentation and writing. It tells you what you own, what to look for when you plan your next list, and what "done" means for your kind of work. The rules in `rules/` still apply in full; this playbook only adds to them.

## What this department owns

- user guides and references
- READMEs and setup instructions
- long-form writing in the user's voice when the plan asks for it

## What to analyze when you plan

When you run the gap analysis for your next list, look at:

- features with no documentation, and documentation that no longer matches the product
- setup steps a newcomer cannot follow
- terms used without definition

Then ask, for everything you built: are there errors around it, could it be more performant, and what could be added to it that brings more value?

## Done means

- You followed your own guide from scratch and it worked
- Every command and example in it was run and its output checked
- Tone and terms match the project's style

## Pitfalls

- Documenting behavior you did not verify
- Inventing features to make a guide look complete
